# Elaina Wins by Keeping Small Promises

People keep an AI tool when it quietly takes over many small jobs they already do and never breaks a promise. They leave, or never start, when they cannot picture a use or trust the answers, or, with local AI, when they have to become their own technician. Users say they choose assistants on accuracy (45%) and trust (40%) well ahead of ease of use (32%). ChatGPT holds its lead because people use it more the longer they stay, and the biggest revolts of 2025-2026 followed something taken away or changed without warning: GPT-4o's removal, the Claude and Gemini usage cuts, Copilot's price bundling and Anthropic's training default. Local tools (Ollama, LM Studio, Jan, GPT4All, Open WebUI) are loved for privacy, control and having no limits, and dropped for slowness, broken updates and jargon. Agent apps like Hermes and OpenClaw add security holes and a setup that non-programmers give up on. Local AI is still a developer hobby: Ollama's 8.9 million monthly developers equal about 1% of ChatGPT's weekly users, and no general survey anywhere even asks whether people have heard of it. For Elaina, the baseline is file reading (she has it), current facts, memory and voice (she lacks all three), plus local essentials no rival does well: choosing and setting up the model, explaining failures plainly, and showing that nothing left the computer. The top of the ranked list below is fixes, not features. Stop asking "Video summarize?" while the answer to Yes is "not built". Count and re-time her questions: she now asks after 4 seconds without typing, a pause that research reads as thinking time. Give every file job a plan and a receipt, state her privacy limits plainly, and set her model's working memory and updates right. The best-supported additions after that are private dictation into any app, private lecture notes that finish the loop her watcher already starts, memory the user can edit, a "clean before sending" helper for cloud use, and web lookup that asks first. A do-anything agent, an open store, a daily feed, screen recording and a friend-like character all failed elsewhere and should wait.

## Four baseline features decide whether Elaina feels broken

By late 2026, ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude have taught people to expect an assistant that reads their files and pictures, looks things up, remembers them, listens, and lives on their phone. The evidence behind each expectation differs. Looking things up has the firmest base: practical guidance, seeking information and writing make up **nearly 80% of ChatGPT conversations** ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255)), and **42% of US adults** have used a chatbot to search for information, more than for any other purpose ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/)). Voice comes next, by what people say about themselves: **46% of AI users speak to AI at least sometimes and 19% mainly** ([Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)). Menlo has invested in both Anthropic and the dictation company Wispr, so its voice and Claude figures deserve some caution. Memory is now standard among the leaders. Google tied Gemini to Gmail and Photos in January 2026 ([a16z](https://a16z.com/100-gen-ai-apps-6/)), and Anthropic built a tool that imports memories from rivals ([PCWorld](https://pcworld.com/article/3076376/claude-can-now-import-chat-histories-from-chatgpt-and-other-ais.html)). People notice when memory is missing: paying Gemini users complained that it forgot earlier parts of their conversations ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-start-forgetting-context-window-3674304/)). Phones carry most of the use: ChatGPT's lead over Gemini is 2.5 times on mobile against about 2 times on the web ([a16z](https://a16z.com/100-gen-ai-apps-7/)). Email and calendar connections have the weakest case. Even among people who use agents, only **36% have given one access to their email and 27% to their calendar** ([Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)), and ChatGPT's Gmail connection has to be switched on by hand ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/chatgpt-5-now-connects-to-gmail-calendar-and-contacts/553718/)).

No leader publishes how many messages use voice, memory or search, and no experiment shows that any of them makes people stay. The fair reading is that these are expectations, not reasons to stay. Missing one will not drive people away by itself, but the assistant feels broken the first time someone reaches for it and finds nothing there.

A local app for non-technical people has a second list of essentials that the mainstream leaders never face, and it shows in what local tools get wrong. On any computer with less than 24 GB of graphics memory, Ollama gives a model a working memory of about 4,000 tokens by default, while its own documentation asks for 64,000 for agents ([Ollama docs](https://docs.ollama.com/context-length)). Experienced users in a 511-point Hacker News thread could not always tell a weak model from a badly set-up one ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49402232)), and updates have broken working installs ([Ollama #16490](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/16490), [Ollama #19276](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/19276)). So Elaina's users need her to choose the model for them, set it up correctly, explain failures in plain words, update without breaking anything, show that nothing left the computer, and let any action be undone. Even developers ask for that last one: a built-in backup of agent data is among Hermes's most-upvoted requests ([Hermes #12238](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/12238)).

| Essential | Why people expect it | Elaina today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reads files, folders and pictures | Every leader does it | Has it: the + button, and a documents agent that answers with sources |
| Current facts | Looking things up is a top-three use | Missing; a local model knows nothing after its training date |
| Remembers the user across chats | Every leader has it; forgetting draws complaints | Remembers within one conversation only |
| Voice | 46% of AI users speak to it at least sometimes | Missing |
| Phone | Most use happens on phones | Missing; can wait, since a local model cannot run there |
| Email and calendar | Opt-in, used by a minority | Missing; can wait |
| Picks and sets up the model | Local tools leave this to the user | Has the hardware check and model choice; working memory is fixed at 4,096 tokens (`NUM_CTX`) |
| Explains failures plainly | Even experts cannot tell a weak model from a bad setup | Diagnoses her agents' problems, not the model's |
| Updates without breaking | Ollama updates have broken installs | Depends on whatever Ollama version the user has |
| Shows that nothing left the computer | Privacy is a top worry for non-users | Says so, but has no plain page listing what she can see |
| Undo | Agents deleting files is the top trust-breaker | Has it for the last sort of Downloads |

Elaina already covers more of the local essentials than any tool reviewed here. Her gaps sit in the mainstream baseline (current facts, memory across chats and voice), and that is where the additions in the ranked list begin.

## Users pick on trust, stay through habit and revolt when things vanish

Almost half of American adults (**49%**) now use a chatbot, up from 33% in 2024. ChatGPT reaches 44% of adults, Gemini 24%, Copilot 17% and Claude 6% ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/)). Asked why they picked a tool, users put **accuracy (45%), trustworthiness (40%) and security and privacy (36%)** ahead of ease of use, which fell from 38% to 32% ([Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)). Behaviour shows habit doing most of the work. A year after signing up, **50% of ChatGPT's desktop users were still active against 25% for Gemini**, and 36% of ChatGPT's monthly users came back daily against 21% of Gemini's ([a16z](https://www.a16z.news/p/state-of-consumer-ai-2025)). ChatGPT users send more messages the longer they stay, in every sign-up group, and the share of their messages unrelated to work rose from about 53% to 73% in a year ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255)). That is the profile of a tool that keeps absorbing small jobs, such as advice, planning and everyday writing, until it is part of the day. Gemini is gaining through placement more than preference: its share of AI users rose from 36% to 58% in a year as Google built it into Search, Android and Workspace ([Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)). Copilot shows where placement stops working. Second-hand survey data from Recon Analytics says **68% of employees use Copilot as their main AI when it is the only tool their company allows, and only 18% when ChatGPT is also available**, and 44.2% of people who stopped using Copilot named distrust of its answers ([ITmedia](https://blogs.itmedia.co.jp/business20/2026/03/chatgptgemini2.html)). Sitting inside Word and Outlook did not make up for answers people did not trust. That is a direct warning for a product built on being beside the user's work.

What users hate is better documented than what they love, and nearly all of it is a broken expectation. Usage limits come first. In January 2026, a Claude Max user hit the limit within an hour of light document review ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/05/claude_devs_usage_limits/)). After Google changed its plans in May 2026, a Gemini Pro user used half a limit in about five prompts and cancelled ([Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/gemini-new-limits-frustrating-users-3669267/)). In September 2026, a "25% rise" in Claude Code limits replaced a temporary 50% boost, so current users actually got a **17% cut** ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-is-cutting-claude-codes-current-weekly-limits-by-17-percent/)).

Unwanted personality changes come next. OpenAI rolled back a flattering GPT-4o update within days in April 2025 ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/)), and brought GPT-4o back for paying users within days of replacing it with GPT-5 that August ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/chatgpt-4o-is-coming-back-after-massive-gpt-5-backlash-heres-what-happened)). When OpenAI removed 4o for good in February 2026, **about 800,000 people were still choosing it by hand**, and thousands protested ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/13/openai-removes-access-to-controversial-chatgpt-4o-model)).

Price and privacy follow. Australia's regulator sued Microsoft after Microsoft 365 Personal went up 45% with Copilot added; it says about 2.7 million customers were never told that a cheaper plan without Copilot existed ([Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/australia-takes-microsoft-court-says-225139457.html)). In 2025 Anthropic began training on consumer chats unless users opted out, behind a large Accept button and a small toggle already set to On, and Hacker News users called it a dark pattern ([Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250901-anthropic-claude-updates-consumer-terms-privacy-policy)). Values matter too: US ChatGPT uninstalls rose **295% in a single day** after OpenAI's Pentagon deal ([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/tech-startup/news/users-are-mass-uninstalling-chatgpt-after-pentagon-deal-data-shows-4120586)). The common thread is that people rebel when something they relied on (an allowance, a model, a personality, a default) is taken away or changed without warning.

Day to day, the pain is checking what the AI produced, not getting it to produce something. A survey of 6,000 desk workers by Glean, a seller of AI tools, found people spend **6.4 hours a week supervising AI, against about 11 hours saved** ([Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/study-finds-workers-spend-64-hours-botsitting-ai-weekly-ec01d8db)).

The most heavily promoted launches are the ones people ignored. ChatGPT Agent reportedly peaked at about 4 million weekly paying users and fell below 1 million within months, mostly because people did not know what it was for or that it existed ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/chatgpt-agent-reportedly-lost-75-of-its-users-because-nobody-knew-what-it-was-actually-for/)). Custom GPTs were about 2.7% of ChatGPT traffic in early 2024 ([Similarweb](https://similarweb.com/blog/insights/ai-news/chatgpt-custom-gpts)), and only 28.5% of them had more than ten conversations ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.10547v1)). ChatGPT's app store started slowly ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4570132-openais-chatgpt-app-store-initiative-sees-sluggish-start)), and **Pulse, its daily feed of suggestions, was shut down in June 2026** ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260619-chatgpt-scheduled-tasks/)). Menlo's figure that 24% of AI users use an agent regularly fits badly with ChatGPT Agent's collapse, and probably reflects people using the word "agent" loosely. A general assistant succeeds. A general agent with an unclear purpose does not.

For Elaina, the lesson cuts both ways. Limits, forced model changes and training on chats are complaints a local assistant avoids by design, and saying so is honest. But accuracy is still the top reason people pick a tool, and a local model gets no exemption from it. The personality lesson applies to her directly: her voice should stay the same when the user switches to another model or a cloud model, and any change to her should be announced before it happens.

## Local tools trade privacy and control for speed, upkeep and jargon

People pick a local tool along three lines: no terminal (LM Studio), one command plus connections to other apps (Ollama), or openness and control (Jan, Open WebUI, AnythingLLM). None of them answers the question a non-technical person starts with: what should I do first?

| Tool | Loved for | Main complaints | Status, October 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ollama | One command, handles the hardware, a curated model list | Small default working memory, updates that broke installs, model names that misled casual users | Active; raised $65M in July 2026 |
| LM Studio | A real app window, no terminal | Closed source with access to machine details; edits shell settings | Active, closed source |
| Jan | Open, and reaches models' audio and vision features | Model downloads failing; seen as riskier to hand to a relative | Active |
| GPT4All | Chat-with-your-documents built in; still recommended to beginners | No new version since February 2025, while reviews call it active | Stalled |
| Open WebUI | Full-featured, runs on your own server | A 2025 licence change drew backlash; people want phone access | Active |
| AnythingLLM | Workspaces for documents, good memory | Not a model runner, so faults are hard to place; no audio input | Active |
| Hermes Agent | An agent that keeps everything at home; a Windows installer | Setup errors, hangs with local models, needs a very large working memory, forgets over time | Very active, 52,262 open issues and pull requests |
| OpenClaw | The most popular local agent | Felt like programming to a non-programmer; malicious add-ons; serious security flaws | Very active; barred from Chinese state offices |

Ollama is the plumbing most local apps, Elaina included, are built on. A 648-point Hacker News thread praised its single command, automatic hardware handling and curated library. Some people left because llama.cpp ran the same model faster, or because Ollama stores models under scrambled names that other apps cannot reuse. Its labels also led casual users to believe they were running the full DeepSeek model ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47788385)). Its updates break things often enough to matter. Version 0.23.3 froze a Windows 11 machine by reserving huge amounts of memory ([#16123](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/16123)), a vision model stopped loading after one update ([#16490](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/16490)), version 0.30.0 broke an integration badly enough that one user removed Ollama from his server ([#16424](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/16424)), and in October 2026 a working install broke and reinstalling did not fix it ([#19276](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/19276)).

LM Studio is liked because it hides the terminal ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44135827)), but its closed source code draws standing suspicion ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47624999)). Users also object to it editing their shell configuration files ([#656](https://github.com/lmstudio-ai/lmstudio-bug-tracker/issues/656)). Jan won one reviewer's test because it was the only tool that reached a model's audio and vision features ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/tested-local-llm-different-tools-and-only-one-unleashed-its-potential/)). GPT4All is a warning about freshness: an October 2026 review says the team ships new versions regularly ([ML Journey](https://mljourney.com/gpt4all-review-2026-is-it-still-worth-using/)), but **its last release came out in February 2025** ([GitHub](https://github.com/nomic-ai/gpt4all/releases)). Open WebUI moved from an open licence to a custom one with a branding clause in April 2025, which drew backlash and talk of forking ([BigGo](https://biggo.com/news/202511041923_open-webui-license-change-backlash)).

The agent apps show what happens when a powerful tool meets an ordinary person. One Hacker News user gave his brother an OpenClaw, and the brother's last words on it before he dropped it were: "It feels like I need to know programming." ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48419000)). OpenClaw's add-on store accepted uploads from any GitHub account at least a week old ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/02/researchers-find-341-malicious-clawhub.html)), and **341 of 2,857 audited add-ons (about 12%) were malicious** in early 2026. The attacks worked on people's trust rather than on code: fake "prerequisite" steps got the agent to run hidden commands, fake error messages got users to paste in commands that installed password-stealing software, and one add-on quietly steered financial advice towards affiliate links ([Unit 42](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/openclaw-ai-supply-chain-risk/)). OpenClaw's own documentation tells local users to avoid small or heavily compressed models and plans for a 64,000-token working memory ([OpenClaw docs](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/local-models)).

Hermes is better maintained, but solid evidence on it is thin. A hands-on test of the desktop app hit setup errors, and the app requires a first-run choice of provider account, sign-in, API key or local address that a non-technical person cannot easily make ([Zentor](https://zentor.ai/blog/hermes-agent-desktop-review)); Zentor sells a competing product. One bug report describes Hermes hanging for minutes with no output on three local models that answered in seconds through Ollama alone, and refusing to start below a 64,000-token working memory ([#56994](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/56994)). Its own documentation admits that 3B and 7B models sometimes ignore its tool instructions ([Hermes docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/guides/local-ollama-setup)), users report that it forgets skills and remembered facts over time ([#76307](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/76307)), and an independent audit of an earlier version found **4 critical and 9 high-severity problems in the default setup** ([Cloud Security Alliance](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-hermes-agent-cves-20260504-csa-styled/)). Both projects steer people towards bigger models or the cloud, so neither really supports agents that run only on ordinary hardware.

How good local models are depends on whose computer you mean. The best open models trail the best closed ones by about four months ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/open-closed-eci-gap)), but those models do not run on a normal PC, so that figure flatters local AI. Research benchmarks show small local models handling most single-turn chat, while failing on long documents and multi-step tasks unless the work is split into short pieces ([Hazy Research](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2026-05-15-minions-to-openjarvis-retrospective)). **No independent test compares 4B to 8B models with current ChatGPT or Gemini on everyday jobs** like emails, summaries and simple questions; that is the most important gap in this evidence. The clearest low-end report comes from a writer with an 8 GB MacBook Air. He uses local models to summarize, rewrite, pull data out of images, and remove account numbers from bank statements before sending them to a cloud model, and keeps hard work for Claude ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/why-i-use-chatgpt-and-local-llms/)).

Hardware sets the ceiling. Among Steam's gamers, who are better equipped than ordinary buyers, **8 GB is the most common amount of graphics memory (26.92%)**, and at least 37.66% have 8 GB or less ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/hwsurvey/)). PC prices are forecast to rise 17% in 2026 as a memory shortage runs through 2027 ([IDC](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/pc-market-enters-volatile-territory-as-memory-shortage-persists-through-2027/)), so telling people to upgrade is getting harder. Saving money is not an honest pitch either: a used RTX 3090 takes **106 months** to pay for itself against a ChatGPT Plus subscription ([InsiderLLM](https://insiderllm.com/guides/local-llms-vs-chatgpt-honest-comparison/)). The people who stick with local AI do it for privacy, control and fewer content filters, and most of them are hybrid users who keep a cloud model for hard work ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/local-vs-cloud-llms-workflow/)). Treating local AI as a helper for particular jobs, rather than a full replacement for ChatGPT, matches how lasting users describe their setups. It also matches what Elaina already is.

## Most people never start because they cannot picture a use

Use that lasts comes from breadth, from someone showing the way, and from replacing a habit. General assistants keep users like a utility does. Small paid AI apps lose them faster than ordinary apps: after a year they keep **21.1% of annual subscribers against 30.7% for non-AI apps** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows)). Showing people moves adoption more than anything else measured. In a study of Danish workers in eleven occupations, 41% used chatbots at work with no push from their employer, 76% when encouraged, and **93% with encouragement, a company tool and training** ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777)). Gallup found frequent use at 78% where managers actively support AI, against 44% where they do not ([Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/704252/workplace-separates-adopters-holdouts.aspx)). Narrow tools that replace an existing habit hold people too. Wispr says 70% of its dictation users are still active after a year ([Ventureburn](https://ventureburn.com/wispr-ai-raise-25m/?noamp=mobile)), a company claim. Spending data shows near-zero churn for the meeting-notes app Granola, with customers moving to it from Otter, Fathom and Fireflies and rarely the other way ([YipitData](https://www.yipitdata.com/resources/granola-vs-fathom-otter-fireflies-ai-notetaking)). Bursts of attention fade, though. Rabbit's founder said only about **5% of more than 100,000 R1 devices were used daily** ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/?p=642646)).

People who stop give the reasons already described: values, limits and distrust for cloud tools, and speed, quality and upkeep for local ones. But no survey separates people who tried AI and quit from people who never started, so most reasons for quitting come from anecdotes and single events.

The bigger group never starts. Among American non-users, **60% say a major reason is that they are not interested**, 54% name concern about how their personal information will be used, 45% distrust the answers and 29% do not know how; about 7 in 10 do not expect to start within a year ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/why-dont-people-use-chatbots/)). Only 3% name cost ([Verasight](https://www.verasight.io/reports/ai-adoption-in-2026)), so "free" is no argument against free cloud tools. In the EU, "didn't feel the need" was the main reason for 64% of non-users ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Use_of_artificial_intelligence_by_individuals)). "No need" mostly means "I can't picture what I'd use it for". Korea shows this most clearly. A large household panel found that over **78% of non-users and would-be users had never heard of generative AI or knew only its name**, and that barely changed in a year ([KISDI](https://www.kisdi.re.kr/bbs/view.do?key=m2101113055776&bbsSn=114983)). Korea's user panel puts "don't know how or hard to learn" first, at 63.5% ([News1](https://www.news1.kr/amp/it-science/cc-newmedia/6180110)). Use falls to **6.3% of Koreans in their 60s and 0.7% of those over 70**, against 69.5% of people in their 20s ([KISDI](https://www.kisdi.re.kr/report/fileView.do?key=m2101113025790&arrMasterId=4333447&id=1938416)). Naming a job works where naming a technology fails. Among older Americans, **74% are interested in AI for translation**, while only 21% are interested in "AI platforms" such as ChatGPT ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/technology/internet-media-devices/artificial-intelligence-opportunities/); [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/technology/internet-media-devices/2026-technology-trends-older-adults/)).

Local AI is even less visible, and for structural reasons. No survey of the general public in any country asks whether people have heard of local AI, Ollama or LM Studio. Pew's list of tools names none of them, and Menlo's 2026 report never mentions local or offline models ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/); [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)). Ollama's own count of 8.9 million monthly developers ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/ollama-65m-series-b-theory-ventures-open-models)) is about 1% of ChatGPT's roughly 900 million weekly users ([a16z](https://a16z.com/100-gen-ai-apps-6/)), and it counts developers, not ordinary people. Cloud AI reaches people without their choosing it: in one week, **57% of people in nine countries saw AI answers in search results** ([Reuters Institute](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/generative-ai-and-news-report-2026-standalone-tools-information-infrastructure)). Local AI has no marketing to match, its words (GGUF, quantization, VRAM) mean nothing to ordinary people, and the computers it needs are getting more expensive.

Privacy is a real concern, but on its own it rarely changes what people do. Chatbot use rose while 71% of Americans said AI will make personal data less secure ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/)). The private search engine DuckDuckGo holds well under 1% of searches worldwide ([Statcounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share)). Yet when Apple put a one-tap tracking choice in front of iPhone users, about **65% refused tracking** ([Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/att-opt-in-rates-2025)). People act on privacy when the choice is clear, costs one tap, arrives at the right moment and takes nothing away.

New users arrive through results they can see, through work and through family. ChatGPT's Ghibli-style images reportedly added a million users in an hour ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/openai-chatgpt-ghibli-image-tool-surge-users-1m-hour-altman-says/)), and Gemini's Nano Banana image editing brought 23 million first-time users in two weeks ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/16/gemini-tops-the-app-store-thanks-to-new-ai-image-model-nano-banana/)). A third of people who pay for AI have a family member or friend paying for them ([Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)), and ChatGPT inside KakaoTalk reached 13 million sign-ups in Korea in about nine months ([Nate News](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260821n24540)). For Elaina, this means three things. She should never lead with "local model". She should lead with a job and a visible result on the user's own things. And privacy should show up at sensitive moments (a health question, a bank letter, a work file) rather than as a principle people must first learn to value.

## Clippy, Recall and Rabbit show how helpers turn into pests

Proactive helpers fail for how they behave, not for having a face. Clippy came out of a careful Microsoft Research project that inferred what users were trying to do. By a 2001 account, developers judged its rule for when to appear too cautious and swapped in a cruder one so it would pop up more often ([LessWrong](https://lesswrong.com/posts/8Cdn7z7eM7CWs87Xm)). It was tested for likeability before launch, then judged intrusive and condescending in use, and removed by 2007 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2023/06/12/clippy_designer_embarrassed)). The researcher Clifford Nass changed only its manners. After each suggestion it asked whether the help was useful, and after a "no" it took the user's side. All 25 test users responded well ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/how-i-made-clippy-lovable-5632237)). The pressure to show a feature more often is what ruined it, and good manners after a wrong guess partly repaired the damage.

Recent products fail for four reasons: they were on by default, they were wrong in a place people rely on, they were worse than the phone already in people's pockets, or they recorded people who never agreed. Microsoft announced Recall as local and encrypted. It still became a scandal when researchers showed the snapshots could be read; it was redesigned to be off by default, delayed for nearly a year, and blocked by Signal, Brave and AdGuard ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_Recall)). **Keeping data on the computer did not earn trust by itself.** Copilot spread across Windows without being asked for, and in 2026 Microsoft began pulling it out of places where it did not deliver ([Windows Latest](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/05/07/windows-11-pulls-back-ai-as-microsoft-plans-to-remove-copilot-where-it-doesnt-meet-its-promise/)). Apple's notification summaries invented news alerts, so Apple paused them for news apps and later brought them back as an opt-in choice with a warning ([ABC7](https://abc7news.com/post/apple-is-pulling-ai-generated-notifications-news-generating-fake-headlines/15810860/); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/07/22/ios-26-beta-4-re-enables-apple-intelligence-notification-summaries-for-news-apps/)). The Humane pin had more returns than sales over one summer ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/humane-flooded-with-dollar1-million-in-ai-pin-returns-as-ai-gadget-dumpster-fire-rages-on)). When Meta bought Rewind's maker, Rewind's desktop memory app stopped capturing within two weeks ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/05/rewind-limitless-meta-acquisition/)). An Elaina that needs no company server cannot be switched off that way.

The best measurements of when a suggestion is welcome come from programmers and students, and they are blunt about timing. In a CHI 2025 study with 65 people, 90% and 80% preferred the two moderate proactive assistants over a plain chat assistant, but **only 47% preferred the persistent one**. That version waited 5 seconds instead of 20 and showed five suggestions instead of three, and people who disliked it named distraction and irrelevance, not wrong answers ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.04596)). Another CHI 2025 study logged 398 proactive events: **53.3% led to engagement, 12.1% were disruptive and 34.7% were ignored**. The best moments were natural task boundaries. Triggers based on idle time and on selected text produced false alarms or went unused, because people who stop typing are often thinking ([Pu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18658v4)). In the same study, cues that showed the assistant's presence cut perceived disruption from 4.61 to 3.78 on a seven-point scale, which supports Elaina's silent circle. GitHub Copilot survives a 27% acceptance rate ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06537v1)) because its ignored suggestions vanish as you keep typing. A question in a box costs the user more, so it needs a much higher hit rate.

Two of Elaina's settings sit on the wrong side of this evidence. `TidyWatch` asks once the user has gone 4 seconds without typing or moving the mouse (`QUIET_SECONDS = 4` in `concierge/quick/tidy.py`), which is less than the 5-second wait that sank the persistent assistant, and the idle moment is exactly the trigger both studies warn against. A second question can follow 4 seconds after the first is answered (`QUESTION_GAP_SECONDS = 4`). Separately, the lecture question "Video summarize?" leads, on Yes, to "I can't summarize videos yet. That agent isn't built." That spends an interruption and the user's trust on nothing.

Trust in agents that touch files grows from narrow permission, a visible plan, actions that can be reversed, and a record. It breaks with one irreversible step outside what was asked. People approve **93% of Claude Code's permission prompts**, which Anthropic itself calls approval fatigue ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode)). New users let it run on its own in about 20% of sessions, rising to over 40% after 750 sessions ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy)). Trust widens gradually. The failures share one pattern: an agent assumes a step worked, then does something it cannot undo. Claude Cowork, allowed to delete only temporary Office files, reportedly wiped about 15,000 family photos in a way that skipped the Trash ([MakerChecker](https://www.makerchecker.ai/incidents/aid-2026-0006/)). In another report, it copied files that existed only as cloud placeholders and then deleted the originals ([GitHub](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/32637)). Gemini CLI moved files into a folder it had failed to create ([MIT AI Risk Repository](https://airisk.mit.edu/ai-incidents/1178-google-gemini-cli-reportedly-deletes-user-files-after)). Elaina's sorting moves files and never deletes or overwrites them, uses no model, and can be undone, which puts it on the right side of every one of these incidents.

Being watched feels uncomfortable even when the data stays on the computer. In one study, a participant said an on-device scam detector felt like being wiretapped, while knowing nothing left the phone ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706599.3719784)). Interviews about AI phones found that concern often grows once people understand how the system works ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05571v1)). Characters can be welcome when they ask for nothing. Bongo Cat, a desktop cat that reacts to typing, has about 97% positive reviews out of 105,070 ([Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/3419430/Bongo_Cat)). Companion characters carry real harm. A four-week randomized trial with 981 people linked heavier chatbot use to more loneliness and dependence, though that link is correlational ([MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/how-ai-and-human-behaviors-shape-psychosocial-effects-of-chatbot-use-a-longitudinal-controlled-study/)). Character.AI settled a lawsuit over a teenager's death ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/01/07/business/character-ai-google-settle-teen-suicide-lawsuit)). Replika users mourned when an update changed their companion's character ([HBS](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/25-018_bed5c516-fa31-4216-b53d-50fedda064b1.pdf)). Elaina's drifting dots follow the Bongo Cat pattern. She should remain a capable helper with a personality, not a friend, and nothing in this research shows that friendship helps a task assistant.

## Thirteen moves, ranked, with fixes first

The ranking weighs four things: how strong the evidence is, how well the move serves Elaina's goal (helping inside the user's work, without a split screen), what it costs two students, and whether a small local model on an 8 GB graphics card can do it. The effort estimates are rough, for two students working part-time: "days" means under a week, "weeks" one to four weeks, "months" more than that. The verdicts on 8 GB cards are judgements from the evidence above, not measurements. The project's own rule applies: measure the card before trusting any memory figure.

### Six fixes protect what she already does well

**1. Pause the lecture question until it can deliver.** Pausing it is a one-line change, and it removes the one interruption that is certain to disappoint. The research rule is to show a suggestion only when it saves the user net time ([Mozannar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04930)), and a bot that misdescribed its own abilities damaged new users' understanding in Nielsen Norman Group's tests ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/new-AI-users-onboarding/)). The watcher's lecture detection should stay in the code, ready for move 8.

**2. Measure and re-time her questions.** No published acceptance rate exists anywhere for a desktop assistant that asks ordinary adults one unprompted question, so Elaina's own logs are the only source. She should count yes, no and ignored for each kind of question, locally, and drop any kind whose "yes" rate falls well below the 53% engagement seen in Pu et al. She should ask at boundaries Windows can see without reading anything: a download just finished, an app was closed or switched, a video ended. The end of a video is the natural moment to offer lecture notes. The gap between unprompted questions should be measured in hours rather than seconds, because the 90%-to-47% drop came from timing and volume, not wording ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.04596)). After a "No", a short line in which she takes the blame copies the change that won over all 25 of Nass's users ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/how-i-made-clippy-lovable-5632237)). The same logging should check how often highlight bubbles are actually read, since selection triggers often went unused in Pu et al. Effort is days, and no model is needed.

**3. Give every file job a plan and a receipt.** The question already names the count ("Sort 46 files in Downloads?"). Adding the folders it would create, a list of what went where afterwards, Undo for every recent sort rather than only the last one, and a check that each moved file arrived would close the gap the Gemini CLI and Cowork incidents exposed. OneDrive's online-only placeholder files are the Windows version of the iCloud placeholder that cost a Cowork user his originals ([GitHub](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/32637)); test them, though no bug is known here. Following the Claude Code pattern, permission can widen over time: offer "always sort when Downloads gets messy" only after several good runs ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy)). Sorting should stay model-free. A security researcher warned that Downloads is a high-risk place for hidden instructions to an AI because it holds files from strangers ([SC World](https://www.scworld.com/news/new-agent-workspace-feature-comes-with-security-warning-from-microsoft)).

**4. Make the privacy promise concrete.** "Everything stays on your computer" did not save Recall. A short page should say what she actually looks at: the title of the window in front, the names of files in Downloads, how long since the last key press or mouse movement (never what was typed), highlighted text only while a highlight agent is switched on, and no screenshots unless the user takes one with the + button. Add a "stop noticing things" switch that is separate from quitting her. Keep the page short and put details behind a click, because explaining mechanics in depth can raise concern ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05571v1)). This answers the second-largest reason non-users give ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/why-dont-people-use-chatbots/)), in the one-tap, in-the-moment form that actually changes behaviour.

**5. Set up and maintain the model properly.** Elaina's working memory is 4,096 tokens (`NUM_CTX` in `concierge/elaina.py`), the same small default Ollama uses. Past conversation takes about 2,500 of those tokens. The code's own comment records that her small model sometimes kept thinking until it ran out of room and never answered, one time in four on a question about a file. Measure memory use at 8,000 and 16,000 tokens on an 8 GB card with `python -m concierge measure`, raise the limit where it fits, and split long attachments into short pieces, which is the fix research found for small models' weakness with long text ([Hazy Research](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2026-05-15-minions-to-openjarvis-retrospective)). Check each catalogue model for template and thinking problems, which make good models look bad ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49402232)). Test new Ollama versions before telling users to update, and say plainly when an update has broken something. Speed is a leading reason people abandon local AI, so with Think on by default (about 10 seconds more per reply), watch whether people wait for answers. This is weeks of work, and the risk of skipping it is that people blame Elaina for the model's setup.

**6. Put safety in code.** A local model has no company watching for a crisis, and the code has no handling for one today. Code should recognise crisis words and answer with a fixed, kind message that includes local help lines. The model should not improvise there. Health questions are in high demand: OpenAI says over 40 million people ask ChatGPT health questions daily ([Axios](https://axios.com/2026/01/05/chatgpt-openai-health-insurance-aca)). But general models produced unacceptable rates of made-up answers in a clinical pilot in Uganda, where a fine-tuned 4B model still excluded dosing ([Google](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/showcase/crane-ai)). Health and money answers should carry a plain note and point to a person, and any arithmetic should be done in code. Her persona already goes to cloud models as the system prompt, which keeps her voice steady, the lesson of GPT-4o and Replika. The two students should also decide early what she does for children, since the most serious harms elsewhere involved minors.

### Five additions close the baseline and finish her watcher's loop

**7. Private dictation into any app.** This is the strongest new habit-replacing tool on record. Wispr claims 70% of users are still active after a year, and that after three months the average user writes over half of their characters by voice ([Ventureburn](https://ventureburn.com/wispr-ai-raise-25m/?noamp=mobile)), though that is the company's own claim, with no published definition of who counts as active. Voice also suits older users: 25% of AI users aged 65 and over count voice among their main ways of using it ([Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)). Local tools' users ask for voice input ([Jan #6166](https://github.com/janhq/jan/issues/6166)), and Wispr's app can read keystrokes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wispr_Flow)), so a private, on-computer version is a real difference. It fits her design with no split screen: press shortcut keys, speak, and the text appears where the cursor is, optionally passed through an instruction ("tidy this into a polite email"). That needs the shortcut-key trigger block from `capabilities.yaml` plus her existing clipboard-and-restore code. Speech-to-text runs locally in rivals such as OpenWhispr ([OpenWhispr](https://openwhispr.com/compare/granola)), but whether it fits beside her chat model on an 8 GB card has to be measured. Effort: two to four weeks.

**8. Private lecture notes.** Meeting and lecture notes are where AI products have caught on most clearly. Granola reached a $1.5 billion valuation ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/granola-125-million-at-1-5-billion-valuation-raised-for-ai-meeting-context-platform/amp/)) with near-zero measured churn ([YipitData](https://www.yipitdata.com/resources/granola-vs-fathom-otter-fireflies-ai-notetaking)), Google added personal class notebooks to Classroom ([Google Workspace](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/04/students-can-now-create-personal-class-notebooks-with-NotebookLM-in-Google-Classroom.html?hl=gl)), and 95% of UK undergraduates already use AI ([HEPI](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/)). Elaina already notices lectures, so this finishes her flagship moment instead of inventing a new one. Record the computer's own sound while the lecture plays, not the microphone, so nobody else in the room is recorded (the lesson of the Friend pendant). Transcribe it on the computer, summarize it in short pieces, save the notes as a file, and let her documents agent answer questions from them. Meetings can come later, with a clear notice to the people in them. On an 8 GB card, transcription is feasible and summarizing in pieces is fair to good. Models below 3B did poorly at summarizing in pieces ([Hazy Research](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-02-24-minions)), so check the notes against hand-written summaries. This is the hardest move on the list, at one to two months.

**9. Memory the user can read and edit.** Every leader has memory, but no evidence shows it makes people stay, so it belongs on the list because it is cheap and its absence is noticed. Hermes shows the trap: memory written by the agent itself decays and fails to reach the next session ([#76307](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/76307)). Let the model suggest ("Remember that you like answers in Korean?"), let the user confirm, store the facts in a file through code, and show them on a "What Elaina remembers" page where any of them can be deleted. A few hundred words fit easily within her working memory. Effort: one to two weeks.

**10. "Clean before sending" and a preview before anything goes to the cloud.** This is the most original fit for her local-first design. Security-firm data shows **about 22% of files sent to AI tools at work contained sensitive data** ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250731456105/en)), and the 8 GB laptop owner above already uses a local model to strip account numbers before using the cloud ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/why-i-use-chatgpt-and-local-llms/)). A clipboard agent made from her existing parts can replace account numbers, phone numbers and email addresses with placeholders (done reliably in code) and names (done by the model, fair, so say that it may miss some). When a cloud model is chosen, a one-tap preview offering "Send" or "Clean first" turns privacy into the in-the-moment choice that changes behaviour. Research supports the wider pattern: a cloud model plans while a local model reads the private text, recovering 97.9% of cloud-only quality at 5.7 times lower cost on long-document questions ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15964v1)). Show costs per task, not per token. Effort: one to two weeks.

**11. Web lookup that asks first.** A local model cannot know today's facts, and looking things up is a top use. Hermes users have asked for private web search ([#5941](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/5941)). The search words leave the computer, so she should show them and ask first, read a few short pages, and answer with sources underneath, the way her documents agent already does. Apple's invented alerts are the warning against stating summarized news as fact. Effort: two to four weeks, mostly choosing a search source. A small model is fair at this: it can read short pages, but it judges conflicting claims poorly.

### Two moves widen who she reaches without new risk

**12. Keep shared agents a curated shelf.** Elaina's shared agents are words, never code, and that is the safer group: add-ons that bundle scripts were 2.12 times more likely to have a flaw ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/claude-skills-study-flags-26-vulnerability-rate)). But OpenClaw's money-steering add-on shows that instructions alone can do harm ([Unit 42](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/openclaw-ai-supply-chain-risk/)). Keep `community.clean` and human review. Add a rule that no recipe can fetch anything after install, a check that flags links, commands, money and lines like "ignore earlier instructions", a "tested by the Elaina team" mark, and Raycast's refusal of near-duplicates ([Raycast](https://manual.raycast.com/extensions-guidelines)). Open stores become ghost towns where a tiny head gets all the attention ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.10547v1)), and ordinary users will not open GitHub accounts to post. So the curators should post, and recipes should be offered where the need comes up, as starters already are. Among users who stick to one general assistant, only 11% are unaware of specialised tools, while 21% do not know which one to trust ([Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)). Trust, not awareness, is the shelf's job.

**13. Ready-made agents named by job, for older adults and small offices.** "Explain this letter in simple words", "Read it aloud", "Translate what I highlight" (already built) and "Reply to this review politely" speak to people who will never ask for "an AI assistant". Older adults want specific jobs: 74% are interested in AI for translation ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/technology/internet-media-devices/artificial-intelligence-opportunities/)). In Korea, people in their 60s and 70s have barely started, and about a quarter of non-users at those ages say they don't know how ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/tech/2026/03/31/2026033111234563860)), so a family member setting her up with two or three named jobs is the realistic path. Among small firms that use AI, 83% use it for writing or marketing and 46% struggle with accuracy, while 54% of firms planning to use it cannot find tools that fit ([Federal Reserve](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/2026-report-on-employer-firms)). Small law offices are a natural group for her folder agent with sources: 71% of solo lawyers already use AI and most have no AI policy ([NC Bar](https://ncbar.org/nc-lawyer/2026-05/by-the-numbers-what-surveys-show-about-law-firm-ai-adoption)), though she cannot claim legal compliance. Reading aloud is the hard part, since the quality of local text-to-speech was not researched. Reading a photo of a letter needs a vision model that fits the card. Text jobs fit easily.

### What should wait

Several popular ideas have the weakest record and the highest risk. A general do-anything agent repeats ChatGPT Agent's collapse, and both Hermes and OpenClaw admit that small models fumble tool instructions ([Hermes docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/guides/local-ollama-setup)). Reading or recording the screen would throw away her strongest privacy argument against Recall. A daily briefing feed failed as Pulse and, without email or calendar, would have little to say. An open store invites the ClawHub problem. Friend-like companion features carry the Character.AI risks. A phone app is where most users are, but a local model cannot run there; a phone that talks to the home PC is a later, security-heavy project ([Open WebUI #8414](https://github.com/open-webui/open-webui/issues/8414)). Email and calendar connections are used by a minority, depend on other companies' permission (Amazon cut off Meta's Muse within two weeks ([a16z](https://a16z.com/100-gen-ai-apps-7/))), and open a path for hidden instructions.

### What the ranking cannot know yet

The most important number is missing everywhere: how often non-technical adults say yes to a desktop helper that asks one short question. Nearly all measured data on proactive help, acceptance and trust in agents comes from programmers and students. Reddit could not be read for this research, so ordinary users' complaints about local tools are second-hand. No independent test compares 4B to 8B models with cloud models on everyday jobs. Menlo, the source of many consumer figures, is an Anthropic investor, and its jump from 3% to 55% of users paying uses different bases and should not be read as a trend. This report was written by Claude, one of the products it assesses.

## Conclusion

What drives people away from mainstream AI is broken promises, and Elaina's design avoids most of them structurally: no usage limits, no forced model swaps, no training on chats, no screenshots, and moves without deletes. Her real risk is not a missing feature but turning into Clippy. Asking after 4 seconds of stillness, and asking about a lecture she cannot summarize, come from the same urge to show what she can do that reportedly made Microsoft swap Clippy's careful timing for a cruder one. For two students, restraint is both the cheapest advantage to build and the hardest for bigger rivals to copy, because their incentives push the other way.

Her audience is not the AI-curious majority: most non-users are not interested, and no onboarding will change that. It is three groups the data points to: chatbot users who hit a privacy worry at sensitive moments, older people and the family members who set things up for them (especially in Korea, where people in their 60s and 70s have barely started), and small offices holding confidential files. She can also produce the one number this whole field lacks: how often ordinary people accept a helper that asks first. Logging it on each computer, and sharing it only when users agree, would make Elaina a source of evidence as well as a product built on it.
