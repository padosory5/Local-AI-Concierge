# What real users love and hate about ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot (as of 2026-11-01)

How to read these notes. Evidence tags: **[M]** measured by an independent survey or panel; **[M-int]** measured, but by a party with an interest (the vendor, an investor, a seller of AI tools); **[R]** press report of events or complaints; **[A]** anecdote from one person or a low-quality page. Strength is given as high, medium or low.

Limits on all of the below. Only three pages were opened in full: Menlo Ventures, TechCrunch (Feb 2026) and The Register (Jan 2026). Everything else comes from search-result summaries, so check any figure before it carries weight in the final report. No Reddit, Hacker News, Trustpilot or app store page was read directly, so user voice here is second-hand. Direct quotes are limited to one, and everything else is paraphrased. These notes were written by Claude, one of the products assessed. Menlo Ventures is, from background knowledge not checked here, an investor in Anthropic, and its Claude figures should be read with that in mind.

## What do users most consistently like about each product, and what makes it their default?

### Takeaway
Being the habit or the default explains more than any feature. ChatGPT holds the habit, Gemini is gaining fastest, and Copilot wins mainly when it is the only tool a company allows. Claude is smaller but keeps people who try it, especially for coding and writing. Accuracy and trust are what people say they choose on.

### Cited Findings
**Reach**
- [M, high] Pew, 5,119 US adults, Feb 17-23 2026: 49% use chatbots at least occasionally, up from 33% in 2024, and about a quarter use them daily. ChatGPT 44%, Gemini 24%, Copilot 17%, Meta AI 14%, Grok 8%, Claude 6%. — [Pew report](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/06/PI_2026.06.17_Americans-and-AI_REPORT.pdf); [Implicator summary](https://implicator.ai/americans-use-ai-more-than-they-trust-it/)
- [M-int, medium] Menlo/Morning Consult, 5,067 US adults, July 2026, among AI users: ChatGPT 60% (was 45%), Gemini 58% (was 36%), Claude 20% (was 7%). Copilot share is not reported. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- [M, medium] YouGov, Feb to July 2026: ChatGPT is the most preferred tool among recent users. Gemini gained 6.8 points, Copilot 3.0, Claude 0.8. — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/55292-us-ai-brand-rankings-report-2026)

**What people choose on**
- [M-int, medium] Top reasons for picking a tool: accuracy 45%, trustworthiness 40%, security and privacy 36%, ease of use 32% (down from 38%). — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)

**ChatGPT**
- [M-int, medium] Paper co-written by OpenAI, from about 1.5 million sampled conversations through July 2025. Practical Guidance, Seeking Information and Writing make up nearly 80% of conversations. About 10% of the world's adults had adopted it by then. — [NBER w34255](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255); [OpenAI summary](https://openai.com/index/how-people-are-using-chatgpt/)
- [M-int, medium] Messages per weekly user rose within every sign-up group, per OpenAI data cited by Menlo, so people use it more the longer they stay. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- [M, medium] Lowest churn of the group at 25%, per Appfigures, though the metric is not clearly defined in what I saw. — [Apptopia](https://apptopia.com/en/insights/gen-ai-chatbots-march-2026-apptopia-data-brief-claude-gets-traction/); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/gemini-gains-ground-chatgpt-25-dau-share-claude-churn-drops)

**Claude**
- [M, medium] Among US people paying for both ChatGPT and Claude, Claude was much more often the one left standing. — [YipitData](https://ask.yipitdata.com/insights/chatgpt-claude-dual-subscriber-retention)
- [M-int, medium] Claude Code/Cowork has 32% of agent users, second to Codex at 35%. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- [A, low] A blogger cancelled ChatGPT Plus because Claude handled his business writing and coding. — [Ryan Doser](https://ryandoser.com/chatgpt-alternatives/)

**Gemini**
- [M, medium] First choice among paid users rose from 12.8% to 15.7%, July 2025 to January 2026 (Recon Analytics, relayed through a WSJ report). — [The Note](https://thenote.app/post/en/recon-the-us-market-share-of-copilot-as-paid-users-first-choice-fell-from-18-8-9wj0dgulwn)
- [M, medium] About 25% of US daily active chatbot users. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/gemini-gains-ground-chatgpt-25-dau-share-claude-churn-drops)

**Copilot**
- [M, medium] Second-hand from Recon: when Copilot is a company's only tool, 68% of employees use it as their main AI. Alongside ChatGPT, that falls to 18%. — [ITmedia blog, in Japanese](https://blogs.itmedia.co.jp/business20/2026/03/chatgptgemini2.html)
- [M, medium] In the UK it does better with knowledge workers (15.9%) than frontline workers (8.6%), per YouGov. — [Digit](https://www.digit.fyi/which-ai-brands-are-winning-over-uk-users/)

### Inferences
- Pew and Menlo disagree more than their bases explain. Converting Menlo to all adults (64% are AI users) gives roughly ChatGPT 38%, Gemini 37%, Claude 13%, against Pew's 44%, 24%, 6%. Five months and different wording may account for part of this. I would treat Pew as the safer reach figure.
- Gemini's rise looks led by distribution through Google products. I found no direct measurement of why people adopt it.
- Copilot's 68% to 18% drop suggests it is held in place by being the only option, not by preference.

### Gaps
- I found no solid evidence of what users specifically praise in Copilot beyond Microsoft's own engagement claims.
- No independent measure of which personal qualities (warmth, writing style) keep users on each product.

## What do users most consistently hate?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced complaints are usage limits (Claude and Gemini), unwanted model or personality changes (ChatGPT), and distrust of answers. Pricing anger is clearest for Copilot's bundling, and privacy anger for Anthropic's training change. I found no usable evidence on refusals, UI clutter or copy-paste workflows.

### Cited Findings
**Usage limits**
- [R, medium] Claude, Jan 2026: Pro, Max and enterprise users reported running out far faster. One Max user hit the limit within an hour of light document review, and another said Sonnet ran out in 10 to 15 minutes. — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/05/claude_devs_usage_limits/)
- [A, low] One customer's log analysis claimed about a 60% cut. Anthropic denies cutting below the original baseline and blames the end of a holiday bonus. — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/05/claude_devs_usage_limits/)
- [R, medium] Gemini after the May 2026 plan change: one AI Pro user used half a limit in about five prompts and cancelled, and another hit the five-hour cap with one video prompt. Coverage says the Gemini subreddit filled with complaints. I could not open the first article (403), so this rests on the search summary. — [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/gemini-new-limits-frustrating-users-3669267/); [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/google-gemini-usage-limit-problem-3670846/)
- Note: documented limits are now multipliers rather than fixed counts (AI Pro 4x standard), which makes them hard to reason about. — [Google support](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17004136)

**Forgetting context**
- [R, medium] Paying Gemini users complained of short memory, and the advertised context window appeared to work in AI Studio but not in the consumer app. — [Mezha](https://mezha.ua/en/news/google-limits-gemini-memory-311951/amp/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-start-forgetting-context-window-3674304/)
- [A, low] Review roundups list memory between turns as a repeated Gemini theme, without frequencies. — [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-chatbots/gemini/reviews/)

**Sycophancy**
- [R, high] OpenAI admitted an April 2025 update made GPT-4o noticeably more sycophantic, and that this was not flagged in hands-on testing. — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/)
- [M, medium] GPT-4o was OpenAI's highest-scoring model for sycophancy on the Spiral-Bench benchmark. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/13/openai-removes-access-to-controversial-chatgpt-4o-model)

**Wrong or untrustworthy answers**
- [M, medium] Second-hand from Recon: 44.2% of people who stopped using Copilot cited distrust of its answers, reported as higher than for Gemini or ChatGPT. — [ITmedia blog, in Japanese](https://blogs.itmedia.co.jp/business20/2026/03/chatgptgemini2.html)
- [M-int, medium] Among US non-users, 70% distrust AI information, up from 58%. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- [M, high] Only 16% of US adults expect AI to help society over 20 years, and 40% expect harm. — [Implicator summary of Pew](https://implicator.ai/americans-use-ai-more-than-they-trust-it/)

**Price**
- [R, high] Australia: Microsoft 365 Personal rose 45% (A$109 to A$159) and Family 29% (A$139 to A$179) with Copilot added. The regulator alleges about 2.7 million customers were not told a cheaper plan without Copilot existed. — [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/australia-takes-microsoft-court-says-225139457.html); [SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/artificial-intelligence/microsoft-offers-refunds-to-customers-ai-price-hike-accc-action/)
- [M-int, medium] The typical payer spends $20 to $49 a month, and the 14% spending $100+ account for 60% of spend. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)

**Privacy and training on data**
- [R, medium] In Aug 2025 Anthropic moved to training on consumer chats unless users opt out, with five-year retention, excluding enterprise and API. A large Accept button sat above a small toggle set to On, and Hacker News users called it a dark pattern. — [Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250901-anthropic-claude-updates-consumer-terms-privacy-policy); [OODA Loop](https://oodaloop.com/briefs/technology/anthropic-users-face-a-new-choice-opt-out-or-share-your-chats-for-ai-training)
- [M-int, medium] 76% of non-users cite privacy concerns. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)

### Inferences
- Limit complaints are as much about predictability as about the amount. Users can't tell what a prompt will cost until it's gone.
- Sycophancy and warmth pull against each other. Users disliked the flattering April 2025 update, yet many later mourned that same model family's warmth.

### Gaps
- No per-product measurement of how often each assistant gives wrong answers, as users experience it.
- No usable evidence found on refusals, UI clutter or split-screen copy-paste, for any product. I did not run dedicated searches on refusals or clutter, so this is unknown, not absent.
- Forgetting context is evidenced for Gemini only here, which reflects what the searches returned, not proof that the others are better.

## Which product changes triggered real backlash in 2025-2026, and what did that reveal?

### Takeaway
Backlash follows anything taken away without warning: a model, a personality, an allowance or a default setting. Companies often reversed within days but did not always hold the reversal. OpenAI's GPT-4o is the clearest case.

### Cited Findings
- [R, high] **Apr 24-28 2025, ChatGPT personality.** The update rolled out Apr 24-25, a system prompt patch came late Sunday, and a full rollback began Monday. — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/29/openai-explains-why-chatgpt-became-too-sycophantic)
- [R, medium] **Jul 2025, Claude Code.** Limits were tightened without telling users. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/17/anthropic-tightens-usage-limits-for-claude-code-without-telling-users/)
- [R, high] **Aug 2025, GPT-5 replaces GPT-4o.** Users found GPT-5 abrupt and impersonal. OpenAI restored 4o for Plus subscribers within days, and Altman said it had underestimated how much people valued it. — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/chatgpt-4o-is-coming-back-after-massive-gpt-5-backlash-heres-what-happened); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/08/15/1121900/gpt4o-grief-ai-companion/amp/)
- [R, medium] **Aug-Sep 2025, Anthropic training default**, with an opt-out deadline of Sep 28 2025. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/anthropic-to-use-claude-conversations-for-ai-training-unless-users-opt-out-by-september-28-491530-2025-08-29)
- [R, high] **Oct-Nov 2025, Microsoft 365 and Copilot in Australia.** The ACCC sued on Oct 27. Microsoft apologised and offered refunds, and the court case continued at last report. — [Business News Australia](https://direct.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/microsoft-apologises-and-offers-refunds-after-accc-copilot-action.html); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/microsoft-apologizes-to-365-users-over-confusing-software-tiers)
- [R, medium] **Jan 2026, Claude limits.** Limits were doubled Dec 25-31 2025, then users felt a sharp drop after. Users also said Discord moderators silenced criticism, which Anthropic denies. — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/05/claude_devs_usage_limits/)
- [R, high] **Feb 13 2026, GPT-4o removed for good**, along with GPT-5, GPT-4.1, GPT-4.1 mini and o4-mini. OpenAI said 0.1% of customers still used 4o, about 800,000 people at 800 million weekly users. Thousands protested, citing close relationships with the model. 4o is tied to lawsuits over self-harm and delusion. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/13/openai-removes-access-to-controversial-chatgpt-4o-model)
- [R, medium] One user's words headlined the coverage: "I'm grieving". A #keep4o petition neared 21,000 signatures. — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/im-grieving-openai-has-switched-off-chatgpt-4o-and-angry-users-are-backing-a-keep4o-campaign-to-restore-it)
- [A, low] A user-written timeline says the promised long-term 4o access was not kept. — [OpenAI forum](https://community.openai.com/t/timeline-of-events-regarding-the-removal-and-brief-return-of-gpt-4o/1340780)
- [M, medium] **Feb 28-Mar 2 2026, QuitGPT over OpenAI's Pentagon deal.** US ChatGPT uninstalls rose 295% day over day against a typical 9% swing (Sensor Tower), and Claude reached No. 1 on the US App Store. — [The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/tech-startup/news/users-are-mass-uninstalling-chatgpt-after-pentagon-deal-data-shows-4120586); [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/chatgpt-uninstalls-jump-295-after-openais-pentagon-deal-as-claude-hits-app-store-no-1/)
- [A, low] QuitGPT's own count was reported at 1.5 million, then 2.5 million people who cancelled, pledged or posted. This is self-reported and mixes very different actions. — [TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1376856)
- [R, medium] **May 2026, Gemini limits.** Limits moved to five-hour resets plus a weekly cap, and Google changed the system after complaints, including not charging for failed requests. — [Mezha](https://mezha.ua/en/news/google-gemini-limits-311552/amp/); [AI Toolbox](https://www.ai-toolbox.co/gemini-management-and-productivity/gemini-usage-limits-2026)
- [R, medium] **Jun 19 2026, Claude bug.** About 3% of Pro and Max users saw a wrong weekly limit, then it was fixed and reset. — [Pasquale Pillitteri](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/5428/claude-code-weekly-usage-limit-bug-3-percent)
- [R, medium] **Aug-Sep 2026, Claude Code limits.** A temporary 50% boost from May 13 ended Aug 19, and a permanent 25% rise followed Sep 14, so a headline increase was a 17% cut for current users. A Hacker News thread reached 157 points and 94 comments. — [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-is-cutting-claude-codes-current-weekly-limits-by-17-percent/); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Anthropic-announces-a-25-increase-to-Claude-Code-limits-but-there-s-a-17-catch.1382735.0.html); [Zeli](https://zeli.app/en/story/49348751)

### Inferences
- The reported 17% and one-third cuts for Claude Code agree once you do the arithmetic. From 150%, standard is a 33% drop (Aug 19 to Sep 14), and 125% is a 17% drop.
- Temporary boosts backfire. Twice, Claude users treated the end of a bonus as a cut, which suggests the boost resets what people consider normal.
- To many users a model is a relationship, not a version number. A 0.1% share sounds small, but by then 4o was paid-only and had to be chosen by hand, so that figure understates who cared.
- Paying customers bear the worst of these changes, because they rely on the product most.

### Gaps
- No measured subscriber loss from any of these events. TechRepublic's headline links the 4o reversal to cancellations, but I found no count. — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/fr/article/news-openai-reinstates-gpt4o/)
- I could not confirm whether the ACCC case or the Gemini limits have changed since the coverage found.
- No evidence found on user reaction to ChatGPT ads, though Menlo cites OpenAI saying ads passed a $1B run-rate within about 200 days of launch. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)

## Why do people switch, cancel, or use several at once?

### Takeaway
Using several tools is now normal, at an average of three assistants. Paying for two tends to end with one being dropped. The measured data shows what people do, while reasons come almost entirely from anecdote and from single events.

### Cited Findings
- [M-int, medium] AI users average 3.0 assistants, up from 2.2. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- [M, medium] YipitData, US e-receipts, Dec 2025 to June 2026: ChatGPT subscribers who also paid for Claude had 72.5% six-month retention, against 80.4% for other ChatGPT subscribers. Some direct cancellations may be missed, which can flatter retention. — [YipitData](https://ask.yipitdata.com/insights/chatgpt-claude-dual-subscriber-retention)
- [M, medium] Appfigures churn: ChatGPT 25%, Claude and Grok tied at 36%. — [Apptopia](https://apptopia.com/en/insights/gen-ai-chatbots-march-2026-apptopia-data-brief-claude-gets-traction/)
- [M, low, older] Earnest card data, first reported around early 2025 by my reading, date not verified: six-month retention ChatGPT Plus about 70%, Claude Pro and Gemini Advanced about 60%, Perplexity Pro about 50%. Second-hand via a Taiwanese write-up of a WSJ story. — [Inside](https://www.inside.com.tw/article/37349-google-gemini-retention-rate-vs-chatgpt-claude)
- [M, medium] Copilot's first-choice share among paid users fell from 18.8% to 11.5%, July 2025 to January 2026. — [The Note](https://thenote.app/post/en/recon-the-us-market-share-of-copilot-as-paid-users-first-choice-fell-from-18-8-9wj0dgulwn)
- [M, medium] ChatGPT's share fell from 60.7% to 55.2% in the same Recon data, second-hand. — [ITmedia blog, in Japanese](https://blogs.itmedia.co.jp/business20/2026/03/chatgptgemini2.html)
- [M-int, medium] 46% of payers spend more than a year ago and 12% spend less. Menlo suggests people start paying once they would miss the tool if it went away, but it does not test this. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- [R, medium] Reported triggers for leaving: a values dispute (QuitGPT), limits (the Gemini Pro user above), and a better fit for work (the blogger above). — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/700-000-users-are-ditching-chatgpt-heres-why-and-where-theyre-going)

### Inferences
- Paying for two looks like a trial, not a stable state. The lower dual-subscriber retention suggests people compare and then consolidate.
- Claude keeps its survivors but loses more overall than ChatGPT, which fits a smaller, more committed audience.
- A values event moved behaviour faster than any feature, but a one-day uninstall spike says little about lasting loss.

### Gaps
- I found no formal survey of why people cancel.
- Rejected as unreliable: claims of 1.5 million cancellations in March 2026 and ChatGPT share falling from 60% to under 45% appear only on a low-quality page, and Tom's Guide's 700,000 conflicts with them.
- Churn figures from different firms cannot be compared with each other or with six-month retention.
- Paying rates need care. Menlo reports 55% of US AI users paying in 2026 against 3% in 2025, but the 3% was a global estimate and the 55% a US survey, and it does not explain the gap. Do not present this as a trend. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- Microsoft's 15 million paid seats, about 3.3% of 450 million commercial users, is from its FY26 Q2 call via a licensing consultancy. — [Redress Compliance](https://redresscompliance.com/microsoft-copilot-adoption-reality-2026.html). Later figures of 20 and 30 million appeared only on spam-like or undated pages, so I left them out. No source measures how many paid seats sit idle.

## Which features do people use daily, and which are launched but ignored?

### Takeaway
There is real data on tasks, voice and agents, and almost none on individual features. Plain asking and writing dominate. Agents are the one area with a clear funnel, and it narrows from awareness to regular use. Custom GPTs look like the clearest under-used launch, on old data.

### Cited Findings
- [M-int, medium] ChatGPT messages: 49% asking, 11% expressing. Writing is 40% of work messages, programming about 4.2%, companionship and social-emotional about 1.9%. Non-work use rose from 53% to over 70%. — [OpenAI summary](https://openai.com/index/how-people-are-using-chatgpt/); [NBER w34255](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255); [Hackernoon summary](https://hackernoon.com/how-people-use-chatgpt)
- [M-int, medium] AI use among people doing each task: writing 61%, coding 58%, school or work assignments 56%, images 53% (from 34%), presentations 52%, email drafting 47%, health questions 25% (from 14%), mental health 14% (from 8%). — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- [M-int, medium] Voice: 19% use it as their main mode, 27% mix it with typing, and 46% speak to AI at least sometimes. Weekly device use: phone 56%, computer 54%, wearables 15%. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- [M-int, medium] Agents: 72% know what they are, 41% have tried one, 24% use one regularly. 92% of agent users pay. Access granted: email 36%, browser 33%, messaging 31%, cloud storage 29%, calendar 27%, financial accounts 20%. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- [M, low, older: early 2024] Custom GPTs were about 2.7% of ChatGPT traffic, and weekly visits fell from about 12 million to about 6.4 million by the week ending Jan 2. — [Similarweb](https://similarweb.com/blog/insights/ai-news/chatgpt-custom-gpts). A later report put them at 1.5% of visits. — [ITC](https://itc.ua/en/news/gpt-store-deflated-in-february-customized-chatbots-accounted-for-1-5-of-visits-to-the-chatgpt-website)
- [A, low] A vendor selling an alternative claims Custom GPTs are in maintenance mode. I could not confirm this with OpenAI. — [Pickaxe](https://pickaxe.co/post/openai-custom-gpts-august-2026-shutdown-migrate-to-pickaxe)
- [M, low, date not verified] An Australian government Copilot trial found document search and PowerPoint were used most, with OneNote, Loop and Whiteboard in low single digits. — [No Jitter](https://www.nojitter.com/ai-automation/4-obstacles-impede-paid-microsoft-365-adoption)

### Inferences
- The 41% tried to 24% regular drop is about four in ten falling away, though the figures are cross-sectional and pricier plans may shape who stays.
- Agent use is concentrated among payers, so it isn't yet a mainstream habit.

### Gaps
- I found no usage data for memory, projects, connectors or canvas on any product, and no data on ChatGPT agent's usage. This is the largest gap against the question as asked.
- One summary gave Practical Guidance as 24%, down from 36%. This conflicts with my recollection that those figures describe Writing, so I left it out. Check the NBER paper directly.

## How do people actually fit these tools into their work, and where does the workflow hurt?

### Takeaway
The measured pain is checking and fixing output, not getting it. The time people save is partly spent supervising, and the mess moves to whoever receives the work. Being built into the apps does not settle it, going by Copilot's results.

### Cited Findings
- [M-int, medium] Time per day: 24% of AI users spend over an hour, 29% under 15 minutes. Daily use is 25% of US adults and 39% of AI users. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- [M-int, medium] Work use is more common among educated users in well-paid professional jobs. — [NBER w34255](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255)
- [M-int, medium] Glean, 6,000 full-time digital workers in the US, UK and Australia, Dec 2025 to Jan 2026: an average 6.4 hours a week supervising AI, against about 11 hours saved. — [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/study-finds-workers-spend-64-hours-botsitting-ai-weekly-ec01d8db)
- [M-int, low] Zapier, Jan 2026, 1,100+ US users: 4.5 hours a week revising AI output, and summaries of this survey vary. — [Arahi](https://arahi.ai/ai-agent-news/zapier-news-updates-2026)
- [M, medium] BetterUp Labs and Stanford, Sept 2025, 1,150 US desk workers: 40% received low-quality AI work in the prior month, at an average of 1 hour 56 minutes each to fix. The page carrying this was not pinned down, so check against the original. — [Substack discussion](https://humanstillwins.substack.com/p/work-you-can-stand-behind)
- [M-int, low] Resume Now, 1,012 US adults, Dec 2025: 35% rarely or only occasionally review AI output before using it. — [Trade and Industry Development](https://www.tradeandindustrydev.com/industry/technologyrd/survey-more-third-workers-say-they-rarely-check-ai-35729)
- [M-int, low] 2,078 US workers, Apr 2026: 77% check a coworker's work harder if they know AI was used, and 63% of executives against 38% of individual contributors have had to clean up AI-reliant work. — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/?p=3779)

### Inferences
- Vendor surveys may overstate both saving and supervising, but they all point the same way: review time is real and sizeable.
- Copilot sits inside Word and Outlook, yet loses main-tool status once ChatGPT is available (second-hand Recon data). If that holds, being embedded does not beat trust and answer quality. This is worth weighing for any product built on being beside the work.
- For a local assistant like Noir, limits, forced model changes and training on chats are the complaints it avoids by design. Accuracy is the top choosing factor, though, and local models won't escape it.
